//About the program

Young people in emerging markets are adopting AI faster than anyone is teaching them to judge it. AI Ethics & Human Skills was built to bridge that gap.

The course sits inside AI to Opportunity, the learning program Goodwall runs for the NextGen AI Alliance. Microsoft, HP and Cognizant founded the Alliance in 2025 to build AI skills for young people worldwide. Accenture joined as a Core Partner to lead its responsible AI track and co-designed this course with us.

The course teaches young people to use AI with good judgment and builds the human skills that make AI useful. Module 1 looks at what AI costs the planet. Module 2 covers how AI fails and how to catch it failing. Module 3 applies human-centered design to a real problem in the learner's own community. Each module names the core human skills it builds, and every badge asks learners to make something themselves.

The course launched on 18 March 2026 and ran to 30 September. It was delivered mobile-first on the Goodwall app and presented by Gen Z creators. In June, we translated Module 3 into Brazilian Portuguese. In August, we added a fourth module that sends learners to Accenture's Learning to Earning (L2E) portal for five free Accenture courses.

//Program objectives

01  Durable human skills alongside AI literacy

Help young people worldwide use AI responsibly and ethically, while building the human skills they need for the future of work: critical thinking, communication, creativity, ethical decision-making and interpersonal awareness.

02  From awareness to practice

Make every credential depend on a demonstrated judgment, such as a quiz answered, an argument made on camera or a solution pitched, rather than on content consumed.

03  Measurable impact at scale

Engage 75,000 young people and credential 10,000, with reach focused on regions where Accenture has a presence.

AI is shaping how we learn, work, and connect. This course helps young learners build the human skills that matter most (like critical thinking, communication, creativity, and ethical decision-making) so that they can use AI responsibly and confidently. Learners explore how AI impacts the planet, how to question AI outputs, and how to use AI to solve real community problems.

//Brazilian Portuguese localization

Brazil was named a priority market at the co-design phase, because underserved communities there have very little access to AI tools and training. In June, Goodwall translated and re-produced Module 3 (AI for Community Problem-Solving & Innovation) in Brazilian Portuguese.

This was a full localization that went well beyond subtitles. A Brazilian creator fronted the trailer, the educational video and the challenge intro. Banners, static ads and all course copy were rebuilt in Portuguese, and the challenge ran under its own hashtag.

Result

Brazil finished as the program's sixth-largest market, with 18,750 young people engaged, ahead of South Africa and the United States. It entered the top 10 for the first time in the 22 July reporting cycle, shortly after the Portuguese module went live. In the final three weeks it grew faster than any other top-10 market, adding 34% more learners between 7 and 30 September, more than double the rate of any other top-10 market.

Credentials have not caught up yet. Brazil converted at 0.3%, with 61 credentials. Only one of the three modules was translated, and it arrived late in the program.

What the curve shows

Engaged crossed its 75,000 target in week 12 and kept climbing. The strongest fortnight was 11–24 June, adding 41,759. Credentialed grew from a much smaller base, and the shape of that curve tells the story of the program's mid-flight correction: 2,835 credentials in the first 14 weeks, and 4,866 in the 14 weeks that followed. Of all credentials, 63% were earned after the design changes made in June. The final three weeks were the strongest of the program. Credentials grew 15.1% while engagement grew 9.4%. In other words, new learners were earning credentials at a higher rate than the program average.

//Observations

Africa converts

Africa delivered 31% of the program's reach and 55% of all credentials. Its credentialing rate was 3.7%, nearly double the program average. These are established Goodwall communities with local ambassadors who promote programs and support learners, and it shows in completion as well as reach.

//Diagnosing the credentialing gap

The program credentialed 7,701 young people against a target of 10,000. The targets assumed that about one in eight engaged learners would earn a credential (13%). The actual rate was 2.0%. Part of that gap comes from reach itself. Engagement came in at five times the target, which pulls the rate down even as the number of credentials grows. Three things explain the rest.

1

The credentialing route was the highest-friction format available

At program launch, every module badge required a self-recorded video, 30–60 seconds long, in which learners made an argument. That was the agreed bar for demonstrated learning. It is also one of the toughest formats we use, and fewer learners complete it than lighter formats such as quizzes.

2

New markets brought reach faster than completion

In established markets like Nigeria, the Philippines and South Africa, local ambassador networks kept learners going through to a credential. In newer markets like India, Brazil and Haiti, we reached large numbers of young people, but fewer stayed for the full journey.

3

Localization opened opportunities in new markets

The program started in English and expanded into Brazilian Portuguese as demand grew in Latin America. Brazil became one of the fastest-growing markets, contributing to 40,964 engaged learners across Latin America and the Caribbean. This gives us a strong foundation to explore further localization in Year 2, particularly in markets where we are seeing strong youth demand, and to test whether a fully localized learning journey can support deeper engagement through to credentialing.

Adjustments to improve credentialing

From June, we made four changes:

  • HP laptops added as prizes for top entries
  • The course prioritized in the Goodwall app
  • Push notifications and newsletter campaigns
  • Two AI image challenges launched in June as easier alternatives to the video challenges, on the same topics

Credentials picked up sharply once these changes landed. The program went from 28% of its credential target in late June to 77% at close, and 63% of all credentials were earned after the changes.

What youth are saying about AI ethics

We went through over 22,000 entries across all challenges and grouped them by theme. Seven patterns emerged frequently enough to report as findings. Together, they show a generation that understands AI's costs and limits better than most people assume, and that puts more weight on its own choices than on rules set by others.

//Summary

AI Ethics & Human Skills engaged 377,228 young people in 205 countries, five times its target. It credentialed 7,701 of them, 77% of target. Along the way, young people shared thousands of original ideas on using AI responsibly in their own communities.

What worked

1

Making the human skills explicit

Naming the human skills developed through each module gave learners a clearer way to understand what they were gaining. It also gave the program a clear framework for measuring impact. This was introduced following Accenture's content review and is now part of Goodwall's standard approach.

2

Asking learners to prove their learning

Every badge asked learners to create something themselves, making the credential journey more demanding than a standard quiz. This added some friction, but it also generated thousands of original youth ideas and responses. These submissions give Goodwall and Accenture a direct view into how young people are applying responsible AI to real problems in their own communities and contexts.

3

Strong local communities carry learners through

Established Goodwall markets, particularly across Africa, converted far better than newer ones, supported by ambassador networks and existing community activity. The program also reached new markets at scale, and those audiences are the next place to build depth.

4

Using the data to improve the program during delivery

We spotted the conversion problem early and made changes during the program, including image generation challenges, hardware prizes, and stronger in-app prioritization. Of all credentials, 63% were earned after these changes, and the program moved from 28% of its Credentialed target in late June to 77% at close.

5

Localization grows reach fast

Brazil became the sixth-largest market within weeks of the Portuguese module launching and kept growing to the end. The next step is turning that reach into credentials, which means translating more in-depth learning paths.

Year 2 opportunities

1

Make the journey to credentialing easier

The video challenge format created a high bar for every credential. In Year 2, we can introduce more variety across the assessment journey, combining formats such as scenario-based questions, written reflections and selected video challenges. This would keep a focus on demonstrated learning while making it easier for more learners to complete the full journey.

2

Localize the full learning journey

Brazil shows that local language grows reach quickly. In Year 2, we should translate the full certificate journey in priority markets, starting where engagement is already strong. That removes language as a barrier to completion and gives credentials the chance to follow reach.

3

Deepen engagement in new markets

Year 1 showed we can reach young people at scale in both established and newer markets. The next step is keeping more of them through to a credential. That means setting targets market by market, balancing paid reach (as in India) with community-led growth, building local ambassador activity in newer markets and bringing more of Accenture's youth and marketing networks into the program.

4

Build stronger pathways from Goodwall into Accenture's wider ecosystem

The L2E module is a starting point. In its first eight weeks, 14% of click-throughs became new L2E profiles. Year 2 can build on this with a clearer journey between the two platforms, supported by stronger promotion through Accenture's youth, marketing and employee networks.

5

Put the best youth ideas to work

The submissions are the program's most valuable output. In Year 2, we can feature the strongest ideas through storytelling, showcases and new challenges, and give selected projects a path to further development. The findings should also shape the content itself: lead sustainability material with water, and show how personal habits and corporate accountability work together. This gives young people a reason to stay connected beyond completing a course.

//Closing

Accenture and Goodwall set out to build the human skills young people need to use AI responsibly and with confidence.

The submissions show what this looks like in practice. In Madagascar, a young woman tests an AI crop diagnosis against other explanations before she trusts it. In Nepal, a young man designs a flood warning built on bells and horns, because they work when the power is out. In Mozambique, an engineer maps waste hotspots next to factories and argues that the companies nearby should help pay for the clean-up.

Year 2 is about giving more young people the chance to do the same, and giving their best ideas somewhere meaningful to go and grow.
